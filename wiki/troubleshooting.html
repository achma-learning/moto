Troubleshooting

Decision trees for common problems. Follow the arrows to diagnose the issue step by step.

Engine Won't Start

  • Is the kill switch in the RUN position?
  • No → Flip kill switch to RUN. Try again.
  • Yes → Continue below.
  • Is battery voltage >12.4V? (Check with multimeter at battery terminals, engine off)
  • No (below 12.4V) → Battery is dead or weak.
  • Charge battery. If it won't hold charge → replace. Check charging system when running (>13V).
  • Yes (>12.4V) → Continue below.
  • Does the starter motor crank? (Key ON, press start button)
  • No crank at all / just a click
  • Check: fuse, starter relay, starter motor connections, side stand switch (must be up), brake lever (some models require brake held). If all OK → starter motor brushes worn or motor seized.
  • Yes, cranks normally → Continue below.
  • Does the engine fire briefly then die?
  • Yes — fires but won't stay running
  • Fuel issue: Check fuel level. Verify fuel reaches carb (disconnect fuel line at carb — fuel should drip when petcock is open). If fuel reaches carb → pilot jet is likely clogged. Clean pilot jet →
  • No — cranks but never fires
  • Ignition issue: Remove spark plug. Ground electrode against cylinder head. Crank — look for spark. No spark → check CDI unit, ignition coil, kill switch wiring. Has spark but won't fire → fuel not reaching cylinder (clogged jet, stuck float, empty tank).
Marrakech Note If the bike sat for >2 weeks in summer heat, the most likely cause is a varnished pilot jet from stale fuel. Drain the float bowl and clean the pilot jet before anything else.

Poor Acceleration / Hesitation

  • At what speed/RPM does the problem occur?

At all speeds

  • General power loss. Check in order:
  • 1. Air filter — clogged filter = restricted airflow = rich mixture = power loss. Clean/replace →
  • 2. Spark plug — fouled or worn plug = weak ignition. Remove and inspect. Replace if electrode is rounded or covered in deposits.
  • 3. Exhaust restriction — carbon buildup in exhaust port or muffler. Common on 4-strokes run rich.
  • 4. Valve clearance — tight valves (esp. exhaust) reduce compression. Check: IN 0.08 mm, EX 0.08 mm cold.

Only at low speed / idle

  • Low-speed fuel delivery issue.
  • Pilot jet clogged (most likely in Marrakech). Clean pilot jet →
  • Also check: vacuum leak at intake manifold (cracked rubber boot), idle air screw adjustment.

Only at high speed / full throttle

  • Top-end issue — CVT or main fuel circuit.
  • 1. CVT rollers — flat-spotted rollers prevent full shift-out. RPM rises but speed doesn't follow. Inspect CVT →
  • 2. Drive belt — worn belt (measure width: replace at <17.5 mm) slips at high load.
  • 3. Main jet — partially clogged main jet starves fuel at wide-open throttle. Clean carb.

Unusual Noise

Ticking / tapping from top of engine

  • Does it change with RPM?
  • Yes, faster ticking at higher RPM
  • Valve clearance out of spec. Check and adjust: IN 0.08 mm, EX 0.08 mm (cold). If clearance keeps going out quickly after adjustment → valve guides are worn. See failure modes →
  • No, constant regardless of RPM
  • Less likely valve-related. Check for loose heat shields, exhaust mount bolts, or fan shroud contact.

Grinding / whining from rear

  • Check gear oil level first — remove fill/check bolt, oil should be at the hole level with bike upright. If low, refill with 80W-90 GL-4.
  • If oil level is fine and noise persists → final drive gear wear. Drain and inspect oil for metal particles. Workshop service required.

Squealing on acceleration

  • CVT belt — belt is worn, glazed, or contaminated with oil. Open CVT cover and inspect. Replace belt if width <17.5 mm or surface is shiny/glazed.
  • If belt is OK → variator or clutch drum surface contamination. Clean all CVT surfaces with brake cleaner (never use oil or grease inside the CVT).

Scraping when braking

  • Brake pads worn to metal. Stop riding immediately. Inspect front disc pads — minimum lining thickness is 1 mm. Metal-on-metal contact damages the disc (expensive to replace).
  • Replace brake pads. Inspect disc surface for scoring — light scoring is normal, deep grooves mean disc replacement.

Overheating

  • How do you know it's overheating?
  • Loss of power, engine pinging/knocking under load, hot smell from engine area, oil burning smell.

Immediate Steps

  • 1. Reduce load — ride at lower RPM, avoid full throttle, get out of traffic if possible.
  • 2. Increase airflow — ride at moderate speed (not idle) to get air over the cooling fins.
  • 3. If engine is pinging/knocking → stop and let it cool for 10-15 minutes.

Root Cause Investigation

  • Cooling fins blocked? — Remove body panels, brush/blow off all dust and debris from cylinder and head fins. This is the #1 cause in Marrakech.
  • Oil level low? — Low oil = reduced heat transfer. Check and top up.
  • Oil old? — Degraded oil loses heat-transfer capacity. If overdue for change, change it now.
  • Fan working? — The forced-air fan on the flywheel should spin when engine runs. If fan blades are broken or shroud is cracked → reduced cooling.
  • Riding pattern? — Prolonged idle in traffic with no airflow is the worst case. On 48°C days, plan routes to avoid extended traffic jams.
  • If all above are OK and still overheating → possible issues: lean fuel mixture (clogged jet), wrong spark plug heat range, internal engine damage. Workshop diagnosis needed.
DTC P0217 — Engine Over Temperature If this code triggers (engine temp >165°C), the ECU has detected a critical overheat event. Do not ignore this. Check all cooling-related items above and change the engine oil immediately — it has likely been thermally degraded.
