How-To Guides

Step-by-step procedures for the most common maintenance tasks. Always work on a cold engine unless noted otherwise.

Engine Oil Change

Interval: Every 2,000 km (Marrakech)  |  Time: ~15 minutes  |  Difficulty: Easy

What You Need

  • 10W-40 semi-synthetic engine oil, JASO MA/MA2 (~800 ml bottle covers a 700 ml change with reserve for top-ups)
  • 17 mm socket/wrench (drain plug)
  • New copper crush washer (M12)
  • Drain pan, funnel, rags

Procedure

  1. Warm the engine — Run for 2-3 minutes. Warm oil drains faster and carries more contaminants out. Don't overheat — just warm.
  2. Position the bike — Center stand on flat, level ground. Place drain pan under the engine.
  3. Remove dipstick/fill cap — This lets air in and helps oil drain faster.
  4. Remove drain plug — 17 mm bolt on the bottom-right of the crankcase. Careful, oil will be warm. Let it drain fully (3-5 minutes).
  5. Inspect the drain plug washer — Replace the copper crush washer every time. They're cheap insurance against leaks.
  6. Reinstall drain plug — Hand-tight plus 1/4 turn with wrench. Do NOT over-torque — the crankcase is aluminum and strips easily. Torque spec: ~25 Nm.
  7. Fill with new oil — Pour ~700 ml through the dipstick hole using a funnel. Check level with dipstick (don't screw it in to check — just rest it). Top up to between the two marks. Capacity: 700 ml at oil change, 800 ml at full disassembly.
  8. Run engine for 30 seconds — Let it idle to circulate oil. Shut off, wait 1 minute, recheck level. Top up if needed.
  9. Check for leaks — Wipe the drain plug area clean and look for drips after a short ride.
Common Mistake Don't confuse the engine oil drain plug (right side, 17 mm) with the gear oil drain plug (left side rear, 12 mm). See Oil Systems page for identification.

Transmission Gear Oil Change

Interval: 1,000 km (first), then every 4,000 km (Marrakech)  |  Time: ~10 minutes  |  Difficulty: Easy

What You Need

  • 80W-90 GL-4 gear oil (~150 ml)
  • 12 mm socket/wrench (drain plug)
  • New aluminum crush washer
  • Small syringe or squeeze bottle (for filling)
  • Drain pan, rags

Procedure

  1. Bike on center stand — Level ground. Place drain pan under the left rear of the engine, near the rear wheel.
  2. Remove the fill/check bolt first — Upper bolt on the left gear case cover. This lets air in and confirms you can fill before you drain.
  3. Remove drain plug — Lower bolt, 12 mm. Let all oil drain (~2 minutes). Note: the volume is small (~120-150 ml), so it drains quickly.
  4. Inspect old oil — Should be dark amber. If metallic gray or has visible shavings, inspect gears.
  5. Reinstall drain plug — New aluminum washer. Torque: 8-12 Nm (workshop manual: 0.8-1.2 kgf·m). Don't over-tighten.
  6. Fill through the check bolt hole — Use a syringe or squeeze bottle. Fill until oil just starts to seep from the check hole with the bike upright. This is the correct level.
  7. Reinstall fill/check bolt — New washer, snug tight.

Air Filter Service

Interval: Clean every 1,000 km, replace every 2,000 km (Marrakech)  |  Time: ~10 minutes  |  Difficulty: Easy

Procedure

  1. Remove the air filter cover — Usually held by screws or clips on the left side of the bike, under the seat/body panel.
  2. Remove the filter element — Pull out the foam element carefully.
  3. Inspect — If the foam is brittle, crumbling, or torn, replace it. Otherwise, clean it.
  4. Clean (if reusable) — Tap out loose dust. Wash in warm soapy water, squeeze gently (don't wring). Rinse until water runs clear. Let dry completely.
  5. Oil the filter (foam type) — Apply a thin coat of foam filter oil. Squeeze to distribute evenly. It should be damp, not dripping.
  6. Reinstall — Ensure the filter seats properly with no gaps. Any unfiltered air = dust in the engine.
  7. Clean the airbox — Wipe out any dust or debris inside the airbox before reinstalling the filter.
Marrakech Tip In heavy dust conditions (sandstorms, construction zones), check the filter more frequently. A clogged filter runs the engine rich, wastes fuel, and fouls the spark plug.

Tire Pressure Check

Interval: Weekly  |  Time: ~2 minutes  |  Difficulty: Trivial

Why Weekly in Marrakech

Extreme temperature swings (cool mornings to 45 °C afternoons) cause pressure to fluctuate significantly. Under-inflated tires wear faster, handle poorly, and increase fuel consumption. Over-inflated tires lose grip on dusty roads.

Procedure

  1. Check when cold — Before riding, or at least 30 minutes after riding. Heat increases pressure by 2-4 PSI.
  2. Use a quality gauge — Pencil gauges are inaccurate. A digital gauge costs 30-50 DHS and is worth it.
  3. Front tire: Inflate to 1.75 bar (25 PSI)
  4. Rear tire: Inflate to 2.0 bar (29 PSI) solo, or 2.25 bar (32 PSI) with a passenger.
  5. Check valve stems — Ensure caps are on. Look for cracking or air leaks at the valve base.
Inspect While You're There Look at tread depth (minimum 1 mm), sidewall cracks, embedded stones or nails, and uneven wear patterns. Uneven wear = alignment or suspension issue.

Carburetor Pilot Jet Cleaning

Interval: Every 3,000 km or at first idle symptoms  |  Time: ~30-45 minutes  |  Difficulty: Medium

What You Need

  • Carburetor cleaner spray
  • Compressed air (can or compressor)
  • Small flathead screwdriver (for jets)
  • Philips screwdriver (float bowl screws)
  • Container for small parts
  • Rags, gloves

Procedure

  1. Turn off fuel — Close the fuel petcock (if equipped) or clamp the fuel line.
  2. Remove the carburetor — Loosen the intake manifold clamp and airbox clamp. Disconnect the choke cable and throttle cable. Remove the carb.
  3. Remove the float bowl — 4 screws on the bottom. Careful — fuel will spill. Catch it in a rag.
  4. Remove the pilot jet — Small brass jet, usually located near the center. Unscrew carefully with a flathead screwdriver. Note its position.
  5. Clean the pilot jet — Spray carb cleaner through the jet from both directions. Hold it up to light — you should see a clean, round hole. If blocked, soak in carb cleaner for 15 minutes and retry. NEVER use wire to poke through — it can enlarge the orifice and change the fuel mixture permanently.
  6. Clean the main jet too — While you're in there, remove and clean the main jet the same way.
  7. Spray all passages — Spray carb cleaner into every visible hole and passage in the carb body. Follow with compressed air.
  8. Clean the float bowl — Wipe out any sediment or varnish. Check the float needle and seat for wear.
  9. Reassemble — Reinstall jets (pilot jet first, snug only — brass strips easily). Reinstall float bowl with all 4 screws evenly tightened.
  10. Reinstall carburetor — Reconnect cables, tighten clamps. Open fuel. Start engine and let idle for 2 minutes to stabilize.
Never Poke Jets With Wire Using a wire or needle to clear a jet orifice will scratch and enlarge it, permanently changing fuel delivery. Only use carb cleaner spray and compressed air.

CVT Belt & Roller Inspection

Interval: Every 5,000 km  |  Time: ~30 minutes  |  Difficulty: Medium

What You Need

  • Socket set (CVT cover bolts)
  • Variator holding tool (or impact wrench)
  • Caliper or ruler (to measure belt width)
  • Rags, compressed air

Procedure

  1. Remove the CVT cover — Left side of the engine. Multiple bolts. Note their positions if different lengths.
  2. Inspect the belt — Look for cracks, fraying, glazing, or chunks missing. Measure width with a caliper: nominal is ~18.5 mm, replace at <17.5 mm.
  3. Blow out dust — CVT case accumulates belt dust and road grit. Clean thoroughly with compressed air and rags. Dust acts as abrasive.
  4. Remove the variator face — Use a holding tool or impact wrench to remove the variator nut. Slide off the front pulley face.
  5. Inspect rollers — All 6 should be perfectly round and identical in shape. Roll each on a flat surface — they should roll smoothly without wobble. Look for flat spots, discoloration, or size differences.
  6. Check the ramp plate — The surface the rollers slide on should be smooth with no scoring or grooves.
  7. Reassemble — If rollers are good, reinstall. If any roller shows a flat spot, replace all 6 as a set. Always use matching weights.
  8. Reinstall CVT cover — Tighten bolts in a star pattern to avoid warping. Don't over-torque.
Roller Replacement Tip Stock roller weight is typically 6-7 grams for the 50cc. Heavier rollers = lower RPM cruising (better for durability). Lighter = higher RPM (more power but more wear). For Marrakech durability, stick with stock or go slightly heavier.

Brake Caliper Piston Service

Interval: Every 6,000 km  |  Time: ~20 minutes  |  Difficulty: Medium

Procedure

  1. Remove the caliper — Two mounting bolts. Support the caliper — don't let it hang by the brake line.
  2. Remove brake pads — Note orientation for reinstallation.
  3. Push the piston out slightly — Use the brake lever to gently push the piston out ~5 mm. Don't push it all the way out or you'll need to bleed the system.
  4. Clean the piston — Wipe the exposed piston surface with brake cleaner and a clean rag. Remove all dust and corrosion.
  5. Apply silicone grease — Thin coat on the piston surface. This protects against corrosion and helps it slide freely. Use brake-specific silicone grease only — never petroleum-based grease near rubber seals.
  6. Push piston back in — Use a C-clamp or large flat screwdriver. Push evenly and slowly.
  7. Clean pad guides and pins — Remove guide pins, clean, apply fresh grease. This ensures even pad retraction.
  8. Reinstall pads and caliper — Pump brake lever a few times to reset pad position before riding.

Throttle Cable Lubrication

Interval: Every 5,000 km  |  Time: ~15 minutes  |  Difficulty: Easy

Why It Matters

Marrakech dust and heat dry out the cable's inner liner. A stiff cable causes jerky throttle response and, in the worst case, cable snap — which means you lose throttle control while riding.

What You Need

  • Cable lubricant spray (or light machine oil)
  • Cable luber tool (optional — makes it much easier)
  • Rags

Procedure

  1. Disconnect the throttle cable at the carburetor end — Loosen the adjuster locknut and slip the cable out of the carburetor throttle lever.
  2. Position the cable vertically — Hold the carb end up so gravity helps the lubricant flow down.
  3. Apply lubricant — If using a cable luber tool, clamp it onto the cable end and spray lubricant in. If not, drip light oil into the cable sheath from the top. Let it soak through.
  4. Work the cable — Pull the inner cable in and out several times to distribute lubricant through the entire length.
  5. Reconnect — Reattach cable to carburetor. Adjust free play: 2-6 mm at the grip. Throttle should snap closed when released.
  6. Test — Turn handlebars full left and right. Throttle should not change RPM in either position. If it does, the cable is too tight or routed incorrectly.
Check Free Play After Lubrication Fresh lubricant can change cable tension slightly. Always verify 2-6 mm free play at the throttle grip. Too little free play = throttle may not fully close = dangerous.

Spark Plug Reading

When: At every spark plug inspection/change  |  Time: ~5 minutes  |  Difficulty: Easy

Why Read the Plug

The spark plug electrode color tells you how the engine is running. It's a free diagnostic tool — the combustion process leaves visible evidence on the plug.

Procedure

  1. Remove the spark plug — Use a spark plug socket. Pull the plug cap straight off (don't twist it).
  2. Examine the electrode and insulator nose — Look at the center electrode tip and the white ceramic insulator around it.
  3. Compare to the chart below — Identify the condition and take action if needed.

Color Chart

Color / AppearanceMeaningAction
Light tan / light brown Ideal — Correct mixture, proper combustion No action needed. This is what you want to see.
White / light gray / blistered Too lean — Not enough fuel or too much air Check for vacuum leaks (intake manifold), clogged fuel filter, air filter missing/damaged. In Marrakech at 460 m altitude, mild lean is normal — only worry if electrode is blistered or melted.
Dark brown / black / sooty (dry) Too rich — Too much fuel or not enough air Check air filter (clogged = rich). Check choke (stuck partially on). If persistent, pilot jet or main jet may be oversized for altitude.
Black and wet / oily Oil fouling — Oil entering combustion chamber Likely worn valve guides or piston rings. Check oil consumption. If burning >100 ml per 1,000 km, internal engine wear is significant.
Heavy deposits / crusty Carbon fouling — Incomplete combustion buildup Usually from excessive idling, short trips, or running too rich for too long. Clean or replace plug. Investigate root cause.
Electrode worn / rounded Normal wear — End of plug life Replace spark plug. Check gap before installing new plug: 0.6-0.7 mm for NGK CR7HSA.
Marrakech Altitude Effect At 460 m elevation with 40°C+ ambient, air is thinner than the sea-level European conditions the carburetor was jetted for. A slightly lean reading (light tan rather than brown) is normal here and doesn't require action unless you see blistering.
