Two Separate Oil Systems

The Kymco Agility 50 4T has two independent oil systems. They use different oil, have different capacities, and are changed at different intervals. Mixing them up can destroy components.

CRITICAL: These are NOT the same oil Engine oil (10W-40) lubricates the piston, crank, cam, and valves. Transmission gear oil (80W-90) lubricates the final drive gears only. They have separate drain plugs, separate fill points, and must never be confused.

Engine Oil

10W-40
GradeAPI SJ or higher, semi-synthetic minimum
Capacity~750-800 ml
Drain plugBottom of crankcase (right side)
Fill pointDipstick/fill cap on right crankcase cover
Change intervalEvery 1,500 km (Marrakech)
CheckWeekly — with bike upright on center stand

Transmission Gear Oil

80W-90
GradeGL-4, 80W-90 gear oil
Capacity~120-150 ml
Drain plugBottom of gear case (left side, rear)
Fill pointFill/check bolt on left gear case cover
Change interval1,000 km (first), then every 10,000 km
CheckAt every engine oil change — just verify level

Why Two Systems?

Unlike most cars (where the gearbox shares engine oil), the Kymco Agility has a CVT transmission that separates the engine from the final drive gears. The CVT belt and variator run dry (no oil). After the CVT, the final drive gears are enclosed in their own sealed case with their own oil. This design means:

  • Engine oil never touches the transmission gears
  • Gear oil stays much cleaner (sealed system, no combustion byproducts)
  • Engine oil gets dirty and heat-degraded much faster
  • Gear oil change intervals are much longer (10,000 km vs 1,500 km)

What Happens If You Mix Them Up?

80W-90 gear oil in the engine Gear oil is far too thick for the engine. It won't circulate properly at startup, starving the cam, valves, and piston of lubrication. Result: rapid wear of cam lobes, valve guides, and piston rings. The engine will overheat and may seize within hundreds of kilometers.
10W-40 engine oil in the gearbox Engine oil is too thin for gear teeth under load. It lacks the extreme-pressure (EP) additives that protect gear surfaces. Result: accelerated gear wear, pitting, and eventually gear failure. You may hear whining or grinding from the rear within a few thousand km.

How to Identify Each Drain Plug

Engine Oil Drain

  • Location: Underside of engine crankcase, right side
  • Bolt size: Typically 17 mm hex
  • Identification: Larger bolt, located forward under the engine block
  • Washer: Copper crush washer — replace at every oil change

Gear Oil Drain

  • Location: Bottom of gear case, left side rear (near the rear wheel)
  • Bolt size: Typically 12 mm hex
  • Identification: Smaller bolt, located further back near the rear axle
  • Washer: Aluminum crush washer — replace at every change

Oil Selection for Marrakech

Engine Oil Recommendations

FactorRecommendation
Viscosity10W-40 — good balance for 5-48 °C range
TypeSemi-synthetic minimum. Full synthetic if budget allows.
API gradeSJ or higher (SL, SM, SN all fine)
JASOMA or MA2 (motorcycle-rated — not "energy conserving")
AvoidCar oils labeled "fuel economy" or "energy conserving" — friction modifiers harm wet clutches (not applicable here, but good habit)

Gear Oil Recommendations

FactorRecommendation
Viscosity80W-90
GradeGL-4 (not GL-5 — GL-5 can be aggressive to yellow metals in some gearboxes)
Quantity120-150 ml — do not overfill (causes seal leaks)
Check methodOil should be level with the fill/check bolt hole when bike is upright
Pro Tip Use a permanent marker to label your oil bottles "ENGINE" and "GEARS" if you keep both in the same storage area. A 30-second precaution that prevents a potentially engine-destroying mistake.
